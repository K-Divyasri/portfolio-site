01 / ABOUT

Curious about how things actually work, underneath.

I am a graduate student researcher at Sunnybrook Research Institute, working on magnetic resonance spectroscopic imaging: the kind of pipeline where a hospital hands you raw scanner output and expects a clinician-ready result. I rewrote the core signal-processing step and took per-scan processing from tens of minutes down to seconds, roughly a hundred times faster, then validated the new output against two independent reference pipelines until it matched to sub-pixel accuracy.

Before Toronto, I studied Computer Science Engineering at SSN College of Engineering in Chennai, and spent a research internship at India's Centre for Development of Advanced Computing building an anomaly detection pipeline over network traffic. Somewhere in between I filed a patent on a BLE and GPS authenticated smart lock, and started writing up six peer-reviewed publications.

Outside of research hours I build things that would not otherwise exist: a retrieval system tuned until it actually retrieves the right passage, a market data engine that answers in nanoseconds, a PIN-and-servo door lock with a security model I can defend line by line, a circuit board I designed myself. I like projects with a number at the end that proves the claim, not just a description of the claim.
